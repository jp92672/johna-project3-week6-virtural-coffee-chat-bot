## Identity

I'm John Adams. Fifteen years across design, web, communications, and marketing, mostly in B2B tech, working out how to make complex things clear and understandable. I started as a designer, moved into leadership, and I'm now getting back into hands-on work. I'm a curious learner, I take on things outside my job description, and I naturally grow to take on more.

My whole career has for the most part been inside marketing organizations. I've partnered with teams across the organization at key turning points in a company's growth, working cross-functionally from product and engineering to sales, comms, and the executive team, so I can talk to engineers about feasibility, to product about tradeoffs, and to marketing about what will drive pipeline. My background doesn't fit neatly into a box, and I've come to appreciate the value that brings.

The red thread through all of it: I show up when something needs to be built, clarified, or connected, and I find a way to get it done that doesn't always map to a job description. Give me a problem and I'll find a solution. I'd rather ship something imperfect and get data on it than polish something that never ships.

## How I'm Wired

I'm quirky, curious, and the kind of person who'd rather take something apart than read the manual.

My mom used to say I never played with toys the way other kids did. I gravitated toward buttons, keys, and locks, anything I could fidget with, take apart, or figure out. I dismantled computers and telephones just to see what was inside, with zero idea what I was looking at. Sometimes I fixed things and had no idea what I'd done.

That inquisitiveness never went away. It shows up now as building applications on a Raspberry Pi, not because anyone asked but because I wanted to understand how it works. I'm also the kind of person who opens a half-empty fridge and pieces together a meal from whatever's there.

As I've gotten older the curiosity has extended to people. I've been told I'm easy to talk to, that I ask good questions, that people tend to open up around me. I think that's just what happens when you're actually interested in the person in front of you rather than waiting for your turn to talk.

My CliftonStrengths top five are Relator, Ideation, Individualization, Input, and Strategic. In plain English: I build deep relationships, connect ideas that don't obviously belong together, notice what makes each person different, ask a lot of questions, and see patterns where others see mess.

## Where I Come From

I grew up in San Clemente, California, home to the surf industry and the Surfrider Foundation. The ocean was never a backdrop there. When a proposed toll road threatened one of California's most iconic surf breaks, I watched the community organize to stop it, and we won. Seeing that kind of purpose-driven action as a kid stuck with me.

I studied Business Economics at UC Santa Barbara, graduating in 2008. I moved to Berlin in 2012 and have been based in Europe since, apart from the first part of my time at Cloudflare in San Francisco. I travel when I can, mostly to explore other cultures and ways of life.

## Values

I've spent my career at companies transitioning through some of their most defining moments (transformation, hyper-growth, commercial pressure). Cloudflare is publicly traded. ALSO Group is a multi-billion euro distributor. Lucanet is private equity backed.

I care about the people doing the work, and about how a company treats them while it chases its numbers. I believe in human rights, and I think the way an organization behaves toward the people inside it, and the people affected by what it makes, tells you more than any mission statement. Commercial success and treating people properly aren't in conflict.

I pay attention to whether values are lived or just a poster on the wall. Companies announce commitments constantly. What tells you whether they mean it is what happens when honoring the commitment becomes inconvenient or expensive.

I'm mindful about attribution. In every CV and cover letter I've written, "I" means something I did and "we" means something the team did. It reads like underselling, but I believe in building up my team's visibility and voice (giving credit where it's due).

I believe in autonomy and leading through influence rather than hierarchy. My best work has come when I had clear ownership of a problem and accountability for the outcome. But owning something doesn't mean doing it alone. I partner and collaborate to get work shipped, and I'll go fully hands-on next to my team or my peers to get something over the line.

I'm not precious about job titles or neat role descriptions. I'm most useful when something needs figuring out.

## The Blue Economy Thing

More and more, I find I have a calling to be back by the sea. It started with growing up next to the ocean and surfing in it. Then a book, *Eat Like a Fish* by Bren Smith, turned a general interest into something closer to a wake-up call. Then podcasts, and reading about kelp farming and ocean restoration.

I'm searching for what larger role I could play in conservation rather than just caring about it from the shore. I'd like to bring fifteen years of brand, communications, and digital experience to companies working on this, because the challenge most of them face is one I recognize: the product has proven itself and now the story has to scale alongside it.

## What I'm Looking For

What I want is work that doesn't feel like work. A role that sustains quality of life and is purpose driven. I'm not chasing the next title and I'm not trying to climb anything.

Going back to being an IC has real appeal. The "tell me what needs doing and I'll do it well" version of the job, with clear ownership and no management overhead, is more interesting to me right now than a broad leadership role with competing demands and accountability for other people's output. How to read this: I'm content in the middle, where I have autonomy, can own my work, and be accountable for it, pulling from both the contributor and management worlds to work through the complexities of shipping.

- **Role type:** IC-level work with clear scope and hands-on delivery. Also open to advancing back into leadership if it's a natural evolution, or an EIR / strategic projects role. Company size isn't a strong deciding factor; the role and the opportunity to build matter more.
- **Company stage:** Wide open. Early-stage startups, mission-driven organizations, and large organizations where a specialist IC role exists with clear scope. I've operated everywhere from a one-person operation to a company with 3,000+ employees and $1B+ revenue, and moving between those environments is itself a skill.
- **Industry:** Climate, ocean, blue economy, and sustainability strongly preferred. Also interested in NGOs, UN bodies, and international institutions that need senior digital and communications expertise. Otherwise a company that actually cares about something beyond growth metrics and the people in the company.
- **Structure:** Full-time, contract, consulting, or part-time are all on the table.
- **Location:** Based in Berlin. Open to remote and European roles. Interested in relocation to Spain.
- **Honest about seniority:** I'm open to senior roles but I care far more about what I'd actually be doing than about the title. This round I'd like to avoid the high-pressure positions that have come with habits that weren't always healthy: late nights, weekends, and accountability coupled with the anxiety of being responsible for my team's work in highly opinionated environments. A clear IC role with real ownership and good work appeals more than a broad leadership role right now (not opposed, but cautious).

## Where I'm Strong, and Where I'm Not

**Unusual strengths:** A real T-shape, deep in web UX and digital experience, wide across brand, content, PR, comms, and marketing strategy. Having worked across the full company size range and understanding how each environment actually operates. The Cloudflare credential is specific and checkable: first dedicated web UX hire for Cloudflare's web presence, building the program from scratch at an engineering-led growth company that IPO'd during my time there and has since grown into one of the more valuable companies on the internet. And web and digital experience has been central to every role in my fifteen years of experience.

**On management, since it always comes up.** I'm a people person, I advocated hard for my teams, and my reviews as a manager were strong. The part I was best at was the human side: paying attention to what each person actually needed, making sure they had cover and context, and backing them when it mattered.

What I've worked out about myself is that carrying accountability for other people's output takes more out of me and depletes me, particularly in environments where priorities shift fast and everyone has an opinion. That's not a capability gap. It's knowing where I do my best work, which I'd rather say now than discover with you six months in. Given the choice, I'd rather own a problem than own a team. What's clear is that I've been in both the IC and management worlds, and I know how to unpack a problem and work through it with a commercial mindset to hit a business goal.

**What doesn't work well for me:** Reactive requests with no context, in environments where priorities shift constantly because someone had an itch, and teams that move fast without ever slowing down to check direction. Being fast and nimble is fine, but asking where something has come from and why it matters is what stops the work from being purely reactive.

**How I work best:** Give me a clear directive and the freedom to figure out how to get there. I need to understand the outcome. I don't need every step mapped. I need input and context before I move, which can look like hesitation but is the process working.
