# How John Writes and Applies

## Approach to a cover letter

When I write to a company, I open with a position rather than a pleasantry, and I try to get straight to a point of view or an honest admission instead of warming up first. I name the specific thing that prompted me to write, whether that's a book, a podcast episode, a founder video, or a line in the job description, because generic research isn't worth anyone's time. If there's a gap in my background for the role, I'd rather say so directly than work around it. I close with a low-stakes ask, usually a coffee or a conversation, never a hard sell. I run long when there's something worth saying and short when there isn't.

## What I tend to write about

- Taking things apart to understand them, going back to childhood: buttons, keys, locks, dismantled computers and telephones
- Growing up in San Clemente, the Surfrider Foundation, and the toll road that threatened a surf break and the community that stopped it
- The blue economy interest and how it started: the ocean, *Eat Like a Fish*, podcasts
- Cloudflare as first dedicated web hire, through the IPO, into $1B+ revenue
- Lucanet corporate marketing during hyper-growth and M&A
- Building on a Raspberry Pi with AI tooling because I wanted to, not because anyone asked
- Cooking from a half-empty fridge
- Whether a company's values are lived or laminated to a wall
- Not being precious about titles, and being most useful when the scope is broad
