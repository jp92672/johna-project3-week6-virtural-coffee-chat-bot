## Intercom – Senior PM, Web (March 2026)

Hello Intercom team,

I'll say this directly because it's relevant: the web has been shitified. Content sections with text on the left and an image on the right, or vice versa, have been the default template for two decades. We've iterated on padding and typography, banished and brought back gradients like this season's fashion trend (thanks, Apple!), all the while the underlying model has remained stagnant. Meanwhile, AI is changing browsing behavior – questions replacing keywords, zero-click search meaning fewer people reach a site the way they once did. And at the same time, AI opens the door to a new frontier. Interfaces that can respond based on input, layouts, and content that adapt in real time, websites that behave less like deep pits of content and more like conversations. That opportunity is here, and it's more exciting than anything that's happened in this space in years. Let's make magic!

TLDR; the rest is where I tell you how great you are and what I bring to the table. Read on if you prefer a long read – otherwise my CV will sum it up just fine 😉

I know Intercom in two different ways. The first is from the outside – the product and design articles, some of the older guides (JTBD), the way the website has always felt a punch above what anyone else was doing. Intercom and Stripe became the references I reached for when agencies asked what good looked like. Not as moodboards, but as proof that websites could have a point of view, a narrative, a sense of craft that made you want to stay and navigate. The second way I know Intercom is through a version I lived myself: Cloudflare, where I spent three years as the first dedicated web hire, then team lead, building the web experience through the company's IPO and into the $1B+ revenue stage. Same drive, different logo. I saw what it takes from the inside, and I've been keeping an eye on Intercom with a very specific kind of recognition.

My path has taken me from designer to leader and a strong pull back toward individual contributor, and that arc matters. Why? I do my best work when I'm working autonomously and accountable for the outcome – leading through influence and trust rather than hierarchy. As a designer, I learned the craft – how to make experiences that were intuitive, functional, and engaging. At Cloudflare, taking on management taught me why decisions get made the way they do: what business pressures shape a roadmap, how to move a cross-functional team, and how much pipeline Marketing needed to contribute to hit our targets. At Lucanet, I owned the digital experience as a strategic platform and later led a corporate marketing function. I've been on both sides of the table. The best individual contribution you can make after leading is categorically different from before. You bring the why back into the how. That's where I am. Not stepping back – stepping in with more to offer.

What I want to bring to this role is exactly that dual lens: web as product, with roadmaps, hypotheses, and measurable outcomes, combined with a genuine sense of narrative and craft. The Senior PM, Web role sits at that intersection – translating positioning into experience, driving cross-functional delivery, holding a high bar for how stories are told and experiences land. I watched Eoghan's video on the Careers page. What struck me was the idea that you need the right people for the type of company you're building, people with the right level of ambition, who work in a mature, engaged, and excited way – people who are going to raise the bar. That's a precise description of how I want to work. No complacency, no half measures, genuinely excited about what's being built and willing to move at the pace it requires.

A company with Intercom's design culture and the ambition to rebuild an entire category around AI feels like the right place for that kind of thinking to actually land.

I'd welcome the chance to talk about where the web team is headed and how I might contribute to it.

Cheers,
John Adams

## Notpla – Speculative (2026)

Dear Notpla team,

I've been curious about the blue economy for a while now, and it often feels like déjà vu. After reading Eat Like a Fish by Bren Smith, my curiosity turned into a deeper wake-up call. Then, while out for a run, I listened to an episode of Material Matters featuring Rodrigo. The podcast didn't just introduce me to Notpla; it made me want to contribute to your story and write this letter.

What struck me wasn't just the product (although seaweed is fascinating). It was the pattern of thinking – starting with one application and following the question outward. From pouches to containers to paper to films to machinery. This approach reminded me of something I recognized from my time at Cloudflare, an idea formed by asking where email spam comes from and ended up becoming one of the most significant infrastructure platforms on the Internet. It was following the red thread that spurred innovation, not the destination.

**What I bring**

Growing up in San Clemente, home to the surf industry and the Surfrider Foundation, I never saw the ocean as just a backdrop. I witnessed our community come together to protect the coastline when a proposed toll road threatened one of California's most iconic surf breaks – and we won. I've been curiously dipping my toes into the blue economy ever since I first put my feet in the sand as a child. What's new is exploring the bigger role I can play in conservation and working toward a healthier planet.

My professional background sits at the intersection of brand communications and digital experience. At Lucanet, I contributed to a significant brand evolution, helping to reposition the company by building external communications and thought leadership and establishing the infrastructure necessary to support them consistently across markets. At Cloudflare, I learned that in B2B, you're never talking to just one person. Buying committees require many different personas to understand something complex in their own way and on their own timeline. Our job is to make complex issues feel clear, relevant, and worth caring about – and to show up in conversation long before anyone is ready to make a decision. Brand awareness, recognition, and recall. You hear a story, file it away, and when the moment comes, you know exactly where to find it.

That's the broader challenge I see Notpla facing: influencing policy, the supply chain, and buyers and consumers who are just beginning to understand what's at stake. It's not the same conversation everywhere, but there's a red thread running through all of it.

**Why Notpla?**

Notpla has reached an inflection point that I recognize: the product has proven itself, and the challenge is to scale the story alongside it. That's where I can add value.

Notpla's credibility is further validated by its win of the Earthshot Prize, its co-founding of the Natural Polymers Group, and its repeated investments from individuals and groups committed to sustainability. This combination of impact-driven and commercial rigor is rare, and it's precisely the type of mission I want to contribute to.

I thrive navigating the unknown. Throughout my career, I've stepped into ambiguity at every stage – from being the first hire and building functions from scratch to aligning cross-functional teams who didn't always speak the same language. The work was never just about the output. It was also about bringing the right people along, having the right conversations, and ensuring that the thinking remained solution-oriented throughout.

I tend to understand things by taking them apart, getting into the mechanics, seeing the system, and following connections between things that seemingly don't belong together. A company with a mission as layered as Notpla's is exactly where I want to be in this next chapter.

I'd love the chance to learn more about where Notpla is headed and have a conversation. If any of this resonates with you, you know where to find me.

Kind regards,
John Adams

Hi there,

I've built a career out of understanding how people and systems work - and finding the space in between where the interesting problems live. You lose people before it's too late.

I pay attention to whether values are lived or just laminated to a wall. The note in the job description about women and people of color being less likely to apply unless they tick every box - small thing, but I noticed it. Keeping that language visible says something.

The web experience and brand caught my eye too. Would be weird if I didn't mention that given my background, hehe.

**Why me?**

When I was young, my mom used to tell me that I never played with toys the way other kids did. I'd gravitate toward buttons, keys, locks - anything I could fidget with, take apart, or figure out. That instinct never really left me. These days it shows up in my daily work and most recently building no code applications (running on my Raspberry Pi) with Claude Code - not because anyone asked me to, but because I wanted to understand how things work. I'm the kind of person who opens a fridge and pieces together a meal from whatever's there.

As I've gotten older, the curiosity has extended to people too. I've been told I'm easy to talk to, that I ask good questions, that people tend to open up around me. I think that's just what happens when you're genuinely interested in the person in front of you rather than waiting for your turn to talk.

All the best,
John Adams

## Voice Notes

This section is safe to keep retrievable. It describes the voice rather than demonstrating it.

**What the real letters actually do:**

- Open with a position, not a pleasantry. The Intercom letter starts with "the web has been shitified." The Seabound letter admits "I don't know the maritime industry" in the second paragraph. Neither warms up first.
- Name the specific thing that caused the letter. A book (*Eat Like a Fish* by Bren Smith), a podcast episode (Material Matters, featuring Rodrigo), a founder video on a careers page, a line in a job description about who is less likely to apply. The research is never generic.
- Use self-deprecation as punctuation, not as a hedge. "lol", "hehe", "Would be weird if I didn't mention that", the occasional emoji. It undercuts the confidence just enough to keep it likeable.
- Admit the gap directly rather than working around it.
- Close with a low-stakes ask. A coffee, a reply, a conversation. Never a hard sell.
- Run long when there's something to say and short when there isn't. The Global Changer letter is a quarter the length of the Intercom one, and that's correct in both cases.

**Recurring material across letters:**

- Taking things apart to understand them, going back to childhood (buttons, keys, locks, dismantled computers and telephones)
- Growing up in San Clemente, the Surfrider Foundation, the toll road that threatened a surf break and the community that stopped it
- The blue economy interest and how it started: the ocean, *Eat Like a Fish*, podcasts
- Cloudflare as first dedicated web hire, through the IPO, into $1B+ revenue
- Lucanet corporate marketing during hyper-growth and M&A
- Building on a Raspberry Pi with Claude Code because he wanted to, not because anyone asked
- Cooking from a half-empty fridge
- "Values lived or laminated to a wall"
- Not precious about titles; most useful when the scope is broad

**What he avoids:** corporate jargon, generic enthusiasm, over-formatting, anything that sounds AI-written, and references to personal struggle or difficult periods.
