**Q: Why did you leave Lucanet?**
A: Honestly, I burned out. Once the private equity investment came in, everything was supercharged transition and growth. We went from around 400 people to 900 while the company was still making the move from startup to midsize, so the organizational maturity hadn't caught up yet. It improved as more experienced people came in, and the marketing leadership took the org from reactive to carrying real revenue targets. But that kind of change comes with storming and norming and a lot of road bumps, and I gave it everything I had. That was two back-to-back runs at full intensity, Cloudflare and then Lucanet. I needed to step back, focus on myself, and upskill before coming back to the market. I'm proud of what I did there, of my team, and it's a great company with a strong ethos on culture. I was grateful to be part of such a pivotal moment in their transformation journey.

**Q: You've moved between IC and management roles. Is that intentional?**
A: I like to go with the flow. I was curious, took on things outside my job description, and I was trusted with more responsibility. Designing taught me the craft. Managing at Cloudflare taught me why decisions get made the way they do: what business pressures shape a roadmap, how to move a cross-functional team, how much pipeline marketing has to contribute to hit the number. At Lucanet I owned digital experience and then a corporate marketing function. So I've seen both sides. Now I want to go back to doing the work, because I do my best work when I'm given a problem to own. The individual contribution you make after leading is different from the one you made before, because you bring the why back into the how. That's not stepping back. It's stepping in with more to offer.

**Q: What's your management style?**
A: I'm not out to be anyone's boss. I'd rather be their partner. If you need advice, clarification, direction, or just another set of hands, I'm there. What I want is for people to own their work and be accountable to it the way I've had to be, shipping end to end. My job is clearing roadblocks. I'm not there to give you the answer, I'm there to help you find it yourself so you develop along the way.

People often say leadership is a lonely path, that you can't make friends or be part of the team because you have to lead it. I disagree. The high-performing teams I've been part of were built on trust, where people could show up as their whole selves and thrive as a unit. Creating that space and valuing the team in it is the manager's job.

**Q: You're a designer who moved into marketing leadership. How do you think about that?**
A: I don't think of them as separate tracks. At Cloudflare the best marketing work came from thinking like a designer: user research, reducing friction, understanding the job to be done. At Lucanet the best design decisions came from thinking like a marketer: positioning, audience, message. What Cloudflare really taught me is that in B2B you're never talking to just one person. Buying committees mean several people need to understand something complex in their own way and on their own timeline, and the job is making complicated things feel clear and worth caring about long before anyone's ready to decide.

**Q: What was your actual scope at Lucanet?**
A: Three roles over three years and four months. I came in as Team Lead for Content and Creation, inherited a team of seven and grew it to nine across content, design, and translation, running a content engine across our key global markets. Then Senior Global Digital Experience Manager, where I led the brand refresh RFP and agency selection, ran the website redesign and Adobe Experience Manager replatform, and consolidated our country-specific domains into a single six-language .com. Then Head of Corporate Marketing, reporting to the VP of Marketing, with a €1.2M budget and a 13-person multi-functional team covering brand, design, content, digital experience, and corporate communications. That last year included directing PR that secured over 4M media reach across Bloomberg, CFO.com, Handelsblatt, and Treasury Today, a flagship industry report, direction on the BARC and IDC analyst reports the team co-authored, and running the external communications for an acquisition and the post-merger brand integration. Worth saying that the marketing organization as a whole, not me, contributed directly to Lucanet's growth from around €100M to €200M ARR over that period.

**Q: What did you actually do at Cloudflare?**
A: I was the first dedicated web and UX hire for cloudflare.com. There was no design program to join, so I built the foundations: workflows, processes, methodologies. The visible output that first year was the homepage redesign, the careers site refresh, the Project Galileo pages, and working with senior executive leadership on the cloudflare.net launch supporting the IPO. A year in I moved into the manager role and built a two-person UX/UI team, led a complete site refresh carrying the brand identity and design system across the whole site, and drove a 25% increase in conversion rate through work on the homepage, pricing, navigation, and the recommendation engine. Three years total, through the IPO and into the $1B+ revenue stage.

**Q: What do you mean by building with AI?**
A: I use AI as a development partner to low/no code even though I'm not a trained engineer. I've built mini-applications running on a Raspberry Pi or Cloudflare Workers, not because anyone asked but because I wanted to understand how the tools work. I write the architecture, the intent, and the decisions. The AI writes most of the implementation. I still read everything and I still have to know when the answer I'm handed is wrong. Code got cheaper to produce, but knowing what to build and describing it precisely did not, and that part has been my job for fifteen years.

**Q: What kind of company culture actually works for you?**
A: Honest and transparent. I've had the luxury of working close to business leaders at both small and large organizations, and the places where I did my best work were the ones where anyone could find a problem, try to solve it, and ship it regardless of their title or role. That freedom to have an impact only holds up when there's a people team behind it that's serious about building somewhere diverse, inclusive, equitable, and worth belonging to. Seeing people as people and not as resources.

**Q: What's your biggest professional failure?**
A: Being conflict avoidant, which is the downside of being a people person. I've avoided tough conversations I should have had, and I haven't always set boundaries, and that's a big part of how I ended up burned out.

There have also been stretches where I was better at following my manager's direction than setting the direction myself. The management skills are established: running a team, unblocking people, keeping work moving. The leadership skills are the ones I still need to continue to develop, meaning holding a vision and taking a team through a strategy I set. That matters even in an IC role, because you should always be applying a commercial lens to your work and what it does for the business.

**Q: What's the most important thing you've shipped?**
A: Two things. At Cloudflare, building the web UX and web presence program from nothing, and getting the company to treat the website as a product rather than marketing collateral. That meant bringing design thinking and human-computer interaction methods to something that hadn't been approached that way before. The aim was to make our web presence Cloudflare's number one salesperson, in the way stripe.com is for Stripe.

At Lucanet, the team transformation. Taking brand, comms, content, and web from a reactive function into a center of excellence that contributed to revenue targets.

**Q: Why the interest in climate and ocean work?**
A: I grew up in San Clemente, which is home to the surf industry and the Surfrider Foundation. When a proposed toll road threatened one of California's most iconic surf breaks, I watched the community organize to stop it, and we won. More recently I read *Eat Like a Fish* by Bren Smith, which turned a general interest into something closer to a wake-up call, and I've spent some time since listening to podcasts and reading about kelp farming and ocean restoration. What's new isn't the caring, it's working out what larger role I could actually play rather than caring about it from the shore.

**Q: Are you open to contract or freelance work?**
A: Yes. I freelanced on and off between full-time roles for about eight and a half years, and I spent two years contracting at ALSO Group embedded in their Cloud Marketplace team, so it's a familiar way of working. I'm interested in finding the right fit, whether that's full-time, contract, or advisory. For the right problem at the right company I'm flexible on structure and not precious about titles.

**Q: Why consider you over someone else?**
A: Because the work you're probably describing doesn't fit neatly into either box. I can design the UX, write the copy, read the data, and ship the code. I can talk to your engineers, your product team, and your executives without a translator. I've also been the first person hired to do a job at a company that had never had it, which means building the function while delivering with it. Whether that fits your specific role is worth a conversation.

A: That's a conversation worth having directly. Want to schedule a call or send a message?

**Q: What do you do outside of work?**
A: Cook, travel, explore the world. Get curious and try to fix or build something around the house (I have our lights automated and I built a garden watering system for our patio, which can all be controlled from an iPhone or Google Home app). Good food, good drink, and good people are my happy places.
